# Meteorological, Agricultural, and Hydrological Drought

## Types and Sequential Progression of Drought
Drought is an extended period of abnormally dry weather caused by significant deficiency of precipitation relative to the long-term climatological normal of a region. Meteorologists and hydrologists classify drought into progressive, interconnected stages:

1. **Meteorological Drought**: The initial phase defined purely by atmospheric precipitation deficits compared to historical baselines over specific temporal aggregation windows (e.g., 30, 90, or 365 days). Standardized metrics such as the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) or percentage departures from normal quantify meteorological severity.
2. **Agricultural Drought**: Arises when persistent meteorological drought propagates into the root zone soil moisture layer, depleting plant-available water below critical physiological thresholds. High temperatures, strong winds, and low relative humidity exacerbate agricultural drought by driving high vapor pressure deficits (VPD) and potential evapotranspiration (PET), inducing plant wilting and crop failure.
3. **Hydrological Drought**: Occurs when prolonged precipitation deficiencies deplete macroscopic water reserves: surface streamflows, natural lakes, reservoirs, wetlands, and subterranean groundwater aquifers. Hydrological drought exhibits significant lag times (often months to years) relative to the onset of meteorological drought.
4. **Socioeconomic Drought**: Manifests when physical water shortages compromise economic supply chains, agricultural commodity markets, hydroelectric power generation, and public drinking water supply.

## Standardized Quantitative Indices
Meteorological monitoring uses objective numerical indices to assess drought intensity:
- **Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI)**: Developed by McKee et al., SPI fits long-term precipitation records to a gamma probability distribution and transforms it to a normal standard deviation scale. Values of -1.0 to -1.49 indicate moderate drought; -1.50 to -1.99 indicate severe drought; and -2.0 or lower indicate extreme drought.
- **Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI)**: Extends SPI by incorporating temperature-driven potential evapotranspiration, making it sensitive to multidecadal atmospheric warming trends.
- **Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI)**: Utilizes a water-balance model balancing precipitation supply against evapotranspiration demand, soil moisture recharge, and runoff.

## Flash Droughts
A rapidly emerging category is the "flash drought"—characterized by unusually rapid onset and intensification over 2 to 4 weeks rather than several months. Flash droughts are typically ignited by anomalous combinations of severe precipitation absence, extreme heat waves, elevated solar radiation, and desiccating winds, which accelerate soil moisture depletion and trigger sudden agricultural stress.
