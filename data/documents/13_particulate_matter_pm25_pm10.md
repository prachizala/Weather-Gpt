# Atmospheric Particulate Matter (PM2.5 and PM10)

## Physical Aerodynamic Diameter Classification
Atmospheric particulate matter (PM) is classified based on aerodynamic behavior and physical size:
- **$\text{PM}_{10}$ (Inhalable Coarse Particles)**: Particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter $\le 10 \mu\text{m}$. Major sources include mechanical crushing, road dust resuspension, construction debris, agricultural tillage, and windblown desert mineral dust. $\text{PM}_{10}$ deposits predominantly in the upper respiratory tract (nasopharynx and trachea).
- **$\text{PM}_{2.5}$ (Fine Respirable Particles)**: Particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter $\le 2.5 \mu\text{m}$. Produced primarily from combustion processes (internal combustion engines, thermal coal power generation, post-harvest agricultural biomass burning, and domestic wood smoke) and secondary atmospheric photochemical reactions forming ammonium sulfates and nitrates. $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ penetrates deep into the alveolar regions of the lungs and can cross the pulmonary blood-gas barrier directly into systemic circulation.

## Meteorological Influences on PM Dispersion
Particulate concentration is heavily governed by the thermodynamic structure of the planetary boundary layer (PBL):
1. **PBL Ventilation and Mixing Height**: Under strong solar insolation and convective turbulence, the planetary boundary layer expands vertically (up to 2,000–3,000 m), diluting surface emissions across a large atmospheric volume.
2. **Nocturnal Temperature Inversions**: During winter nights under cloud-free anticyclonic conditions, intense terrestrial radiation cools the ground surface faster than the overlying air, producing a thermal inversion where temperature increases with height. This creates an extremely stable atmospheric "lid" (mixing heights dropping below 100–200 m) that traps surface pollution, causing extreme smog episodes frequently observed in the Indo-Gangetic Plains.
3. **Wet Deposition / Rainout**: Precipitation effectively scavenges aerosol particles from the atmosphere via droplet impaction, producing dramatic post-rain air quality improvements.
