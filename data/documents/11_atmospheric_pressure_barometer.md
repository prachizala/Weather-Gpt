# Atmospheric Pressure Systems and Barometry

## The Hydrostatic Concept
Atmospheric pressure represents the gravitational force exerted by the weight of the air column above a unit area of Earth's surface. At standard mean sea level (MSL), international standard atmospheric pressure is defined as:
$$1013.25\text{ hPa (hectopascals)} = 1013.25\text{ millibars} = 760\text{ mmHg} = 29.92\text{ inHg}$$
Atmospheric pressure decreases quasi-exponentially with altitude (falling approximately 1 hPa for every 8.5 meters near sea level). Consequently, meteorological stations report pressure adjusted to Mean Sea Level (QNH or MSLP) to remove topographic bias and permit analysis of horizontal pressure gradients.

## Highs vs. Lows: Kinematics and Weather
Horizontal variations in atmospheric pressure drive global and synoptic winds via the Pressure Gradient Force (PGF), modified by Coriolis deflection and frictional drag:
- **Low-Pressure Systems (Cyclones / Depressions)**:
  - Surface winds converge inward (counterclockwise in the Northern Hemisphere).
  - Mass convergence forces upward vertical motion (ascent) of air.
  - Ascending air cools adiabatically, leading to condensation, cloud formation, precipitation, and stormy or unsettled conditions.
- **High-Pressure Systems (Anticyclones)**:
  - Surface winds diverge outward (clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere).
  - Upper-level convergence causes downward vertical motion (subsidence).
  - Descending air warms adiabatically, evaporating cloud moisture and yielding light winds, clear skies, dry conditions, and strong nocturnal radiative cooling (often forming surface temperature inversions and fog).

## Barometric Tendency as a Predictive Diagnostic
The rate of change in atmospheric pressure over a 3-hour period (barometric tendency) is one of the oldest and most reliable physical indicators in observational meteorology:
- **Rapidly Falling Pressure (> 2 to 3 hPa / 3 hours)**: Approaching frontal boundary, tropical depression, or convective squall line; deteriorating weather imminent.
- **Rapidly Rising Pressure (> 2 to 3 hPa / 3 hours)**: Passage of a cold front, onset of gusty post-frontal clearing, or approach of an anticyclonic ridge.
- **Steady Pressure**: Stable, persisting synoptic weather regime.
