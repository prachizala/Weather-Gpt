# Wind Chill Equivalent Temperature

## Thermal Boundary Layer Disruption
In calm or stagnant cold air, a micro-layer of warm, insulated air molecules (the thermal boundary layer) naturally adheres to the exterior of exposed human skin due to convective heat dissipation from the body. As wind speeds increase, convective turbulence continuously strips away this boundary layer, replacing it with fresh cold air molecules. This substantially accelerates the rate of conductive-convective heat transfer from the body to the ambient atmosphere.

## Operational Definition
The Wind Chill Equivalent Temperature (WCET) is not a true physical temperature measurable with a traditional thermometer. It represents the hypothetical air temperature in calm winds (approx. 4.8 km/h or 3 mph) that would produce an identical rate of heat loss from exposed facial tissue as the prevailing combination of ambient temperature and wind speed. 

Because wind chill reflects convective heat loss toward the surrounding air temperature, an inanimate object (such as a vehicle engine or exposed water pipe) can never drop below the actual ambient air temperature regardless of how fierce the wind blows, although it will cool down to that air temperature much more rapidly.

## Thresholds for Frostbite Risk
- **-10°C to -24°C (14°F to -11°F)**: Uncomfortable cold; low risk of frostbite for normal outdoor exposure.
- **-25°C to -34°C (-13°F to -29°F)**: Moderate risk; exposed skin can freeze in 10 to 30 minutes.
- **-35°C to -50°C (-31°F to -58°F)**: High risk; frostbite occurs on exposed skin in 5 to 10 minutes.
- **Below -50°C (-58°F)**: Extreme life hazard; frostbite can occur within 2 to 5 minutes of direct atmospheric exposure.
