# The South Asian Summer Monsoon (Southwest Monsoon)

## Meteorological Driving Mechanisms
The South Asian summer monsoon (occurring from June through September) is the planetary scale wind reversal that provides over 75% of India's annual precipitation. The system is propelled by coupled thermal and dynamic drivers:
1. **Differential Land-Sea Heating**: During boreal spring and early summer, intense solar insolation heats the vast Eurasian and Indian landmass far more rapidly than the surrounding Indian Ocean, establishing a sharp regional thermal and barometric gradient.
2. **Elevated Heating of the Tibetan Plateau**: The Tibetan Plateau (average elevation ~4,500 m) acts as a colossal elevated heat source, radiating sensible and latent heat into the middle troposphere. This induces intense regional convective lifting and anchors the upper-tropospheric Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ).
3. **Northward Migration of the ITCZ**: The Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) migrates northward toward the foothills of the Himalayas, creating the "Monsoon Trough" characterized by low sea-level pressure.
4. **Cross-Equatorial Flow and Somali Jet**: Southeasterly trade winds in the Southern Hemisphere accelerate toward the Mascarene High (near Madagascar), cross the equator off the coast of East Africa, and turn northeastward under the Coriolis force as the low-level Findlater Jet (Somali Jet), transporting immense moisture over the Arabian Sea.

## Onset and Spatial Trajectory
The Southwest Monsoon formally onsets over the state of Kerala around June 1 (with standard deviation of ~7 days), monitored by criteria set by the India Meteorological Department (IMD) regarding rainfall depth across designated stations, outgoing longwave radiation (OLR), and westerly wind depth up to 600 hPa.

The monsoon advances across the subcontinent via two primary geographical branches:
- **Arabian Sea Branch**: Strikes the Western Ghats (producing heavy orographic precipitation along the windward Konkan and Malabar coasts while leaving the Deccan Plateau in rain-shadow), advancing northward through Gujarat and central India.
- **Bay of Bengal Branch**: Traverses the warm waters of the Bay of Bengal, curves counterclockwise around the Arakan Mountains of Myanmar and the Meghalaya Plateau (triggering world-record rainfall at Cherrapunji and Mawsynram), and travels westward up the Indo-Gangetic Plains.
