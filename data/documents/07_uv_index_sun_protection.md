# The Global Solar UV Index

## Physics and Formulation
The Global Solar UV Index (UVI) was established by the World Health Organization (WHO), World Meteorological Organization (WMO), and United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) as an international standard measurement of sunburn-producing (erythemal) ultraviolet radiation at the Earth's surface. 

Solar UV radiation encompasses UVA (315–400 nm), UVB (280–315 nm), and UVC (100–280 nm). Earth's stratospheric ozone layer completely absorbs UVC and approximately 90% of UVB. The UV Index is calculated by weighting spectral irradiance against the McKinley-Diffey erythemal action spectrum (accounting for the higher cellular damaging efficiency of shorter UVB wavelengths), integrated across the UV spectrum, and scaled by a factor of 40 m²/W to yield a dimensionless index.

## Environmental Modifiers
- **Solar Zenith Angle**: UVI peaks at solar noon when the sun is highest in the sky and path length through the atmosphere is minimal.
- **Altitude**: UV intensity increases by approximately 10% to 12% for every 1,000 meters of elevation gain due to thinner atmospheric scattering.
- **Surface Albedo**: Fresh snow reflects up to 80% of UV radiation (nearly doubling exposure); white sand reflects 15%; water reflects 5–10%.
- **Cloud Cover**: Thick overcast stratus diminishes UV radiation significantly, but thin cirrus or broken cumulus can occasionally augment UV exposure via lateral cloud scattering.

## WHO Exposure Categories and Protection Protocols
- **Low (UVI 0–2)**: Minimal danger for the average person. No protective action required outdoors; wear sunglasses on bright days.
- **Moderate (UVI 3–5)**: Moderate risk of erythema from unprotected sun exposure. Seek shade during midday hours, apply broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen, wear wide-brimmed hats.
- **High (UVI 6–7)**: High risk of sunburn. Reduce sun exposure between 10 AM and 4 PM, wear photoprotective clothing.
- **Very High (UVI 8–10)**: Very high risk. Unprotected skin burns rapidly (within 15–25 minutes for fair skin types). Take full protective precautions.
- **Extreme (UVI 11+)**: Extreme danger. Unprotected skin and eyes can burn in less than 10 minutes. Frequently observed in tropical latitudes (including peninsular India) and high-altitude alpine regions.
