# The Beaufort Wind Force Scale

## Historical Origin and Standardization
Developed in 1805 by British naval officer Sir Francis Beaufort, the Beaufort Wind Force Scale provides an empirical, observational framework relating wind speeds to observed terrestrial and maritime phenomena. In modern operational meteorology, it is standardized by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO).

## Scale Classification
- **Force 0 (Calm, < 1 km/h / < 1 knot)**: Smoke rises vertically; mirror-like sea surface.
- **Force 1 (Light Air, 1–5 km/h / 1–3 knots)**: Direction of wind shown by smoke drift, not by wind vanes.
- **Force 2 (Light Breeze, 6–11 km/h / 4–6 knots)**: Wind felt on exposed face; leaves rustle; ordinary vanes moved by wind.
- **Force 3 (Gentle Breeze, 12–19 km/h / 7–10 knots)**: Leaves and small twigs in constant motion; light flags extended.
- **Force 4 (Moderate Breeze, 20–28 km/h / 11–16 knots)**: Dust and loose paper raised; small branches move.
- **Force 5 (Fresh Breeze, 29–38 km/h / 17–21 knots)**: Small trees in leaf begin to sway; crested wavelets form on inland waters.
- **Force 6 (Strong Breeze, 39–49 km/h / 22–27 knots)**: Large branches in continuous motion; whistling heard in telegraph wires; umbrellas used with difficulty.
- **Force 7 (High Wind / Moderate Gale, 50–61 km/h / 28–33 knots)**: Whole trees in motion; inconvenience felt when walking against the wind.
- **Force 8 (Gale / Fresh Gale, 62–74 km/h / 34–40 knots)**: Breaks twigs off trees; generally impedes progress.
- **Force 9 (Strong Gale, 75–88 km/h / 41–47 knots)**: Slight structural damage occurs (chimney-pots and slates removed).
- **Force 10 (Storm / Whole Gale, 89–102 km/h / 48–55 knots)**: Seldom experienced inland; trees uprooted; considerable structural damage.
- **Force 11 (Violent Storm, 103–117 km/h / 56–63 knots)**: Very rarely experienced; accompanied by widespread damage.
- **Force 12 (Hurricane Force, $\ge$ 118 km/h / $\ge$ 64 knots)**: Devastating structural and ecological destruction.
