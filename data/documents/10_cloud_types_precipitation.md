# Cloud Genera and Precipitation Physics

## The Ten Principal Cloud Genera
Clouds are classified under the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) International Cloud Atlas according to altitude and morphological appearance:

1. **High Clouds (Base > 6,000 m / 20,000 ft)**: Composed entirely of ice crystals.
   - *Cirrus (Ci)*: Delicate, detached fibrous wisps.
   - *Cirrocumulus (Cc)*: Small, thin white rippled patches.
   - *Cirrostratus (Cs)*: Transparent veil causing optical halo phenomena around the sun or moon.
2. **Middle Clouds (Base 2,000–6,000 m / 6,500–20,000 ft)**: Composed of water droplets and ice.
   - *Altocumulus (Ac)*: Gray or white sheets of roll-like elements.
   - *Altostratus (As)*: Grayish, fibrous uniform sheet diffusing the sun as through ground glass.
3. **Low Clouds (Base < 2,000 m / 6,500 ft)**: Composed predominantly of liquid water.
   - *Stratocumulus (Sc)*: Gray patchy rolls with dark tessellated sections.
   - *Stratus (St)*: Uniform, featureless gray fog-like layer producing drizzle.
   - *Cumulus (Cu)*: Detached, dense, sharp-outlined mounds with flat bases ("fair-weather cumulus").
4. **Clouds of Multi-Layer / Vertical Development**:
   - *Nimbostratus (Ns)*: Thick, dark, widespread continuous rain-producing cloud.
   - *Cumulonimbus (Cb)*: Massive thunderstorm cloud tower extending to the tropopause with an anvil-shaped fibrous top, producing severe turbulence, lightning, torrential downpours, and hail.

## Physical Mechanisms of Droplet Growth
Cloud droplets have typical diameters around 10 to 20 micrometers ($0.01\text{–}0.02\text{ mm}$), possessing terminal fall velocities too minuscule to overcome buoyant atmospheric updrafts. To fall as rain ($d \ge 0.5\text{ mm}$), droplet mass must increase by a factor of roughly one million via two physical processes:
1. **Collision-Coalescence Process ("Warm Rain")**: In clouds warmer than 0°C (common in maritime and tropical environments), larger droplets fall faster than smaller ones, sweeping up and coalescing with smaller droplets along their trajectory.
2. **Bergeron-Findeisen Process ("Cold Rain")**: In mixed-phase clouds between 0°C and -40°C containing supercooled liquid water droplets and ice crystals, saturation vapor pressure over ice is significantly lower than over liquid water. Consequently, water vapor sublimates rapidly onto ice crystals while liquid droplets evaporate, causing ice crystals to grow rapidly into precipitation-sized snowflakes that melt into raindrops on descent through warmer lower layers.
